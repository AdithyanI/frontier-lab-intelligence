## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored, not AI slop—but badly in need of an editor.** The custom signal funnel, screened-network vocabulary, rank-versus-judge distinction, and exact proof links are unmistakably built for Frontier Lab Intelligence. The risk is AI-like over-completeness: the same five-stage story appears as scrollytelling, a long written report, a final report, a five-Insight showcase, and a seven-row rubric map.

The deterministic detector returned zero findings in `frontend/src/features/system/HowItWorksPage.tsx`. That is useful but narrow: it confirms the absence of its known code-level anti-patterns, not that the information architecture is disciplined.

Parent browser inspection at 1280×720 covered the opening, the sticky funnel, the written-method transition, the technical middle, the five-Insight proof set, and the closing rubric map. The live page measured 14,916 vertical pixels, 20,276 rendered text characters, 84 paragraphs, 29 headings, 19 buttons, and 26 links. No browser warnings or errors were present.

## Overall Impression

The first half feels distinctive, intelligent, and professionally restrained. The custom funnel is a real visual thesis, not dashboard decoration. The single biggest opportunity is to trust that thesis: stop after the funnel has done its job and route the reviewer directly into one excellent cited Insight. The long second explanation makes the authored experience feel generated by accumulation.

## What’s Working

1. **Product-specific visual concept.** A broad field of public activity narrows into separate Investment and AI Engineering briefs. The imagery and vocabulary could not be moved unchanged into generic SaaS.
2. **Defensible separation of concerns.** Choose → Collect → Rank → Judge → Publish clearly explains why attention ordering is not truth and why audience judgment is separate.
3. **Real proof.** Dates, counts, suppressions, unit costs, live-surface links, and five exact Insights demonstrate a working system rather than decorative architecture.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The strongest story is repeated until it feels unedited

The funnel consumes roughly six viewport heights before the page restarts as “The same funnel, in words.” Six method chapters, a final report, five showcases, and the rubric map follow. Preserve the funnel, then show a compact audit path and the five best Insights. Move plumbing and full rationale behind deliberate deep-dive navigation.

Suggested command: `$impeccable distill`

### [P1] Accessibility structure does not match the visual craft

The page jumps from `h1` to `h3`/`h4`. The fullscreen figure has no initial focus, focus trap, focus restoration, or semantic close button; clicking anywhere closes it. Use proper `h2` sections and a native or fully managed dialog with a visible close control. Make small “next” controls at least 44px high.

Suggested command: `$impeccable audit`

### [P1] The first proof action sends the reviewer away

The opening says the external video is the fastest explanation. That weakens the product-native walkthrough before the live evidence proves anything. Lead with a 60-second in-product action: open one Insight and trace it to its evidence. Keep video secondary.

Suggested command: `$impeccable layout`

### [P2] Inline funnel and diagram labels are too faint and small

At the inspected desktop viewport, important SVG copy is visually tiny and low-contrast, while expansion is not persistently advertised. Reduce embedded copy, increase rendered type, and keep a visible “Open figure” control.

Suggested command: `$impeccable typeset`

### [P2] Trust claims overshoot adjacent proof

“Nothing has to be taken on trust” is rhetorically stronger than provenance can support. Validation numbers such as the rank correlation and conversion rates lack adjacent audit destinations. Prefer “Every published claim links to preserved evidence,” and attach method/snapshot links to key validation figures.

Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`

## Cognitive Load

High overall: four checklist failures. The scrollytelling itself is focused and well grouped, but the complete route fails single focus, compact chunking, minimal choices, and progressive disclosure. The written restart is the main emotional valley.

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex, power user:** no visible “skip to proof,” no chapter index, and “Why this choice” jumps far down-page without a reciprocal return. The five-Insight proof arrives after the method and plumbing.

**Sam, keyboard/screen-reader user:** skipped heading levels, incomplete dialog focus behavior, inaccessible close label, globally intercepted arrow keys in the funnel, and tiny SVG labels.

**Leonie, BIT external reviewer:** the first recommended action opens Descript; the strongest evidence is near the bottom; repeated first-person justification begins to compete with the product; exact metrics without adjacent audit links create skepticism where trust should peak.

## Minor Observations

- “Two briefs, fully traceable” is the strongest emotional ending and should govern what follows.
- The five-Insight list and rubric map are valuable; they should be reachable immediately, not buried.
- “Almost everything is noise” is memorable but more absolute than necessary.
- The visual system is clean, consistent, and materially above generic generated UI.
- Smooth jumps do not update the hash or move focus, weakening shareability and recovery.

## Questions to Consider

1. After 90 seconds, should the reviewer have understood the architecture or verified one excellent cited Insight?
2. If the page is the figure, why does the opening say the video explains it better?
3. Which is the real ending: the traceable briefs, the five-Insight proof set, or the rubric map?
4. Could every material validation number link directly to the snapshot, method, or live surface that produced it?
