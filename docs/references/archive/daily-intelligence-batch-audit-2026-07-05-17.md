## Recurring editorial weaknesses

### Tail selection and ranking

Busy days often retained every defensible item rather than the material items
a BIT reader would genuinely want to know. The clearest cases were 7 and
10 July. Rank sometimes over-weighted severity or novelty while under-weighting
source quality, portfolio directness, and decision consequence.

The main lesson is not to impose a numeric cap. The missing gate is marginal
decision value: what changes for this reader after including this Insight, and
is that change stronger than the next candidate?

### Cross-day novelty

The same NVIDIA announcement supported near-identical Investment conclusions
on 5 and 6 July. The Apple complaint reappeared on 10 and 11 July without a new
ruling, remedy, or other decision-relevant development. Event IDs differed, so
within-day identity validation could not detect the repetition.

Future work should expose compact recent-development fingerprints—canonical
source URLs, accepted Insight IDs, company/mechanism keys, and the prior core
claim—and require the editor to state what is new. It should not inject prior
Insight prose wholesale because that would anchor the next editor.

### Evidence support and chronology

No selected frozen artifact was disclosed after its brief day. The frozen
manifests therefore passed the temporal audit. Durable imported provenance is
still incomplete: many artifact and web citations do not record a publication
date, and evolving web pages can be historically plausible without making
their historical state independently reproducible.

Validation proves that an artifact excerpt exists in the frozen text. It does
not prove that the excerpt supports every material clause written around it.
The audit found gaps in NVIDIA credit-risk detail, Muse distribution claims,
trace-mining metrics, Inkling reasoning-effort claims, and SPACE scale claims.
These were citation-selection failures, not necessarily missing underlying
evidence.

### Scope consistency

Current-stack relevance was applied inconsistently. Some self-hosting, audio,
or future-surface experiments were selected while analogous candidates were
rejected because FLI does not operate those surfaces. Investment occasionally
included general portfolio news or outside-company context without a strong
frontier-AI transmission path.

## Investment impact-label calibration

Across the current 5–17 July Investment reader there are 49 Insights and 57
company read-through rows:

| Label | Company rows | Share |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `positive` | 30 | 52.6% |
| `negative` | 14 | 24.6% |
| `mixed` | 8 | 14.0% |
| `uncertain` | 5 | 8.8% |

Only three of the 48 mapped Investment Insights contain only `uncertain`
company mappings. The current 17 July run has four mappings: one positive, one
negative, and two mixed.

This is intentional under the current contract: generic exposure, a product
launch, or a plausible competitive mechanism does not prove financial impact.
The caution is useful, but the distribution makes the label weak as a scanning
signal and conflates two different states:

1. a defensible directional hypothesis whose realization is unproven; and
2. no defensible net direction at all.

The wording and instruction calibration succeeded without a schema migration.
The field now represents a **potential directional read-through**: realization
and magnitude remain in the key uncertainty and watchpoints, while `uncertain`
is reserved for genuinely indeterminate direction. The resulting distribution
was not targeted; each mapping still requires a concrete causal mechanism.

## Agent feedback

Every date-specific task wrote a retrospective after its validated import.
Recurring requests were unusually consistent:

- 11 of 11 wanted a compact, read-only coverage or preflight ledger with one
  row per Event/audience pair.
- 10 of 11 wanted compact and consistent `import-result` or `inspect-run`
  projections rather than full stored prose.
- Most wanted exact artifact text windows, disclosure time, citation skeletons,
  and better historical web-availability capture.
- Several wanted batch company matching and complete nested schema examples,
  especially for `affected_entities`.
- Repeated `Bad file descriptor` failures occurred under high process and task
  concurrency. The imported editorial state remained isolated and consistent,
  but unbounded eleven-way execution overloaded the host control plane.

The feedback supports better inspection surfaces, not automated editorial
choice. Grouping, selection, rank, causal direction, and company impact remain
judgments.

## Follow-up implementation status

1. **Implemented:** a compact `preflight` review ledger showing pair
   coverage, rank/title preview, Event roles, affected entities, citation
   excerpts, disclosure eligibility, and missing or duplicate status. Omit
   prior annotations by default.
2. **Deferred:** cross-day development fingerprints and a required novelty delta without
   exposing prior prose as an authoring anchor.
3. **Implemented:** compact, consistent `inspect-run` projections; the full
   stored payload remains the backward-compatible default.
4. **Deferred:** exact source-text windows and historical-availability metadata for
   citation review.
5. **Deferred:** bounded unattended Codex concurrency of roughly three or four
   tasks per host.
6. **Implemented and calibrated:** potential-direction wording on 17 July,
   followed by a current-harness rerun of 16 July.
